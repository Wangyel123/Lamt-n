# Draft notes for the idea sheet (1 page; rewrite and personalise)

## Who the users are
- **School counsellor** (main user): one person, many students, little time. Needs to know who to see first.
- **CECD supervisor**: needs patterns across schools to plan support, without seeing any child's identity.
- **The student** (indirect, but the reason for all of this): answers honestly only if they trust what happens next.

## Top 3 pain points
1. **Who do I see first?** Answers sit in a spreadsheet; the most at-risk child can be row 47.
2. **Who have I already met?** Follow-ups live in notebooks and memory; nothing shows what's still open.
3. **Reporting upward without exposing children.** Supervisors need numbers; small groups make children identifiable.

## Ideas for a first feature (5+)
1. **Counsellor flag board**: everyone sorted by priority, with the reason in plain words.
2. **Follow-up tracker**: status and history for every flagged student.
3. **Supervisor dashboard**: counts by school, small numbers hidden.
4. **Survey runner**: students answer on a phone with one-time codes.
5. **Data-quality checker**: catches blanks and typos before they hide a child.
6. **Referral log**: who was referred where, and when.
7. **Overdue reminder**: nudges when an Urgent case hasn't been met within a day.

## The one I picked, and why
**The flag board with follow-up tracking.** It turns data the schools already collect into action on day one, for the person closest to the child. It also forces the right foundations (rules, privacy, audit trail) that every other feature needs.

## One thing in the brief I'd challenge
**"Prefer not to say" on self-harm falls through to no flag.** 7 of the 8 students who chose it get no flag under the baseline rules. A skipped sensitive question is a signal worth a gentle check-in. I raise them to High (not Urgent), switchable, and explain why.

*(Also worth a line: "<5" is ambiguous, either group size or any small count. I took the stricter reading. And flags must never become labels on a child: the board says plainly that flags are a screening aid, not a diagnosis.)*
